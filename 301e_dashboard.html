StaffingUnion vs. Non-Union Staff — Trade-offs and Prevailing Wages
▼

Current status: 301-52 operates with non-union staff. This provides scheduling flexibility and generally lower total compensation costs compared to a SEIU 32BJ unionized building.

Non-union prevailing wages (NYC, 2026 est.):

RoleHourly RangeAnnual Equivalent
Live-in Superintendent$28–$38/hr$58K–$79K + apt
Porter / Handyman$22–$30/hr$46K–$62K
Part-time Doorperson$20–$26/hr$20K–$30K (part-time)

Union (32BJ) comparison: Wages are roughly 15–25% higher than non-union, plus mandated benefits (health, pension, paid leave) adding ~$8–12K per FTE annually. Total cost premium for a fully unionized building: +$25K–$45K/year for a 16-unit building.

Considerations for unionizing: Would require board vote, shareholder communication, and negotiation with 32BJ. Once unionized, reversing is very difficult. Benefits include standardized procedures and union dispute resolution. Not recommended for a building this size unless there are specific staffing management challenges.

ComplianceLL97 Carbon Emissions — What It Means for 301-52
▼

Local Law 97 (2019) sets building carbon emissions caps with fines beginning in 2024. For most NYC residential buildings, the compliance path involves reducing gas heating, improving insulation, and upgrading to electric or heat pump systems.

  • Fines: $268/ton of CO₂ over the cap
  • 2024–2029 caps are relatively lenient; 2030+ caps are strict and will require capital investment
  • 301-52 uses gas heating (Heating $26,910 YTD, ~$54K/year) — a significant emissions source

Recommended next steps: Commission an LL87 energy audit (if not already done — due for buildings over 25K sq ft, but worth doing anyway). Engage an MEP engineer to model emissions and estimate cost of heat pump or high-efficiency boiler upgrade. Budget $50,000–$200,000+ depending on current baseline.

Reserve implication: LL97 compliance cost should be modeled in the Forecasting tab. Start with a $75K placeholder for 2028-2029 and adjust based on engineering assessment.

  • Day 31–60: Halstead late notice; management alerts treasurer

PlanningReserve Study — Should 301-52 Commission One?
▼

A reserve study is a professional assessment of the building's physical condition and the funds needed to repair or replace major components over time. It covers roof, façade, mechanical systems, elevators, common areas, and more.

Why now: LL11 façade work is already underway. The engineer engaged for LL11 may be able to expand scope to a full reserve study at marginal additional cost. Cost: $3,000–$8,000 for a 16-unit NYC co-op.

Benefits: Sets a defensible capital plan; improves lender confidence at 2033 mortgage renewal; gives shareholders a credible long-term view of assessments needed.

NY State note: Reserve studies are not legally mandated for co-ops (unlike some condo structures), but Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac now require reserve fund adequacy disclosures. Recommend commissioning one in the next 12 months.
